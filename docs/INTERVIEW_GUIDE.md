# Interview guide: spoken journals, students and journalists

Goal: find out whether the niche has a real problem *today*, before building more. Aim for 5 to 10
conversations per niche, 20 minutes each. Listen more than you talk.

## Rules

- Ask about what they **did** last time, not what they **would** do. Past behaviour is evidence; opinions about a future app are not.
- Do not pitch MURMUR in the first 15 minutes. Do not lead ("wouldn't it be useful if…").
- Ask to see their phone: how they record, where the notes live, how they look something up.
- Take notes with their own words. Write down exact phrases.
- Never record the interview without their explicit consent, and do not keep what they say about private matters.

## Questions for everyone

1. Tell me about the last time you recorded your voice for yourself. What happened, step by step?
2. What did you use? Why that one?
3. What happened to that recording afterwards? Have you listened to it again? When?
4. Think of a time you needed to find something you had said in an old recording. How did it go?
5. What do you do today to turn speech into text? How often?
6. Is there anything you would not record because of who could get it? What would change if you were sure nothing leaves your phone?
7. What have you tried and given up on? Why?
8. Do you pay for anything in this area now? What and how much?

## Spoken journals: extra

- How often do you talk (or write) your journal? What makes you skip a day?
- Where are the older entries? Do you ever go back to them?
- Who else could see your phone or cloud account? Does that influence what you say?

## Students and journalists: extra

- How long are your usual recordings? In which language(s)? Noisy places?
- What do you do after recording: re-listen, type it up, paste it somewhere?
- How do you handle a source or classmate who does not want to be on a cloud service?
- What would a wrong word in a transcript cost you?

## After the interview

Show the library and a note's transcript only now (use the demo build). Ask: "What would you do next with this?" and "What is missing for you to use it this week?"

## Reading the results

For each person, mark: **has the problem now** (did it in the last month), **uses a workaround** (what), **would switch** (said so *and* did something, e.g. joined the beta list). Five people with all three in one niche is a signal; compliments are not.
